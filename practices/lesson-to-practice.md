# From Lesson to Practice

This practice defines how **lessons are converted into practices** in CIMP.

A practice does not originate from opinion, preference, or convention.
A practice exists only as a result of learning.

---

## Statement

A PRACTICE must only be created
when a lesson reveals a **repeatable risk**
that cannot be reliably prevented by awareness alone.

If no structural change is required,
no practice is justified.

---

## Origin of a Practice

A practice may originate only from:

- an INCIDENT_LESSON
- a RETRO that produced generalized knowledge
- repeated near-misses indicating a systemic gap

A practice must be traceable to its origin.

Practices without an explicit lesson are considered provisional.

---

## When a Lesson Becomes a Practice

A lesson requires a practice when at least one of the following is true:

- the same class of failure is likely to recur
- the failure bypassed existing constraints
- the risk depends on human attention or memory
- the failure was detected too late
- the cost of repetition is unacceptable

If repetition is harmless or unlikely,
formalization is not required.

---

## Nature of a Practice

A practice is a **normative rule**.

A practice:
- states what must or must not be done
- protects a specific invariant
- applies to future changes
- is independent of a single incident

A practice is not:
- an explanation
- a post-mortem
- a suggestion
- a reminder

---

## Enforceability

A practice is considered complete only if it is enforceable.

Enforcement may take the form of:
- a checklist
- a review requirement
- an automated validation
- a schema or runtime constraint

A practice without enforcement is advisory.
Advisory practices do not prevent recurrence.

---

## Advisory vs Enforced Practices

Advisory practices:
- document awareness
- rely on attention and discipline
- decay over time

Enforced practices:
- change system behavior
- survive team rotation
- reduce reliance on memory

Only enforced practices are considered durable.

---

## Failure Mode

A common failure mode is to stop at documentation.

This occurs when:
- lessons are recorded but not formalized
- practices exist but are not enforced
- enforcement is optional or implicit

In this case, the system has not learned.

---

## Relationship to the Change Learning Loop

This practice applies to the following transition:

```
INCIDENT_LESSON
↓
PRACTICE
```

It exists to ensure that learning results in
**structural change**, not just understanding.

---

## Final note

Systems do not fail because lessons are unknown.

They fail because lessons are not turned into rules.

This practice exists to close that gap.
