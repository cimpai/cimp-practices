# Retro as Observation

This practice defines the role of RETRO in CIMP.

A RETRO exists to capture **what happened**,
not to decide **what to do**.

---

## Statement

A RETRO is an **observational artifact**.

Its purpose is to record:
- outcomes
- deviations
- surprises

not to assign responsibility
or prescribe corrective actions.

---

## Position in the Learning Loop

A RETRO occupies a single position in the Change Learning Loop:

```
CHANGE_PLAN
↓
RETRO
↓
INCIDENT_LESSON
```

It exists between execution and learning.

If this position is skipped or overloaded,
learning becomes unreliable.

---

## What a RETRO Captures

A RETRO records:

- what was expected
- what actually occurred
- where reality diverged from intent
- what signals were missing or misleading

It may include:
- timelines
- observations
- metrics
- qualitative notes

It must not include decisions.

---

## What a RETRO Does Not Do

A RETRO does not:
- define fixes
- propose practices
- assign blame
- optimize processes

These actions belong to later stages.

When decisions are made inside a RETRO,
observations are distorted.

---

## Relationship to Lessons

A RETRO may produce:
- no lesson
- one lesson
- multiple lessons

This is acceptable.

A RETRO that produces no lesson
is still valid if it captured reality accurately.

A lesson without a RETRO is considered weak.

---

## Failure Mode

A common failure mode is collapsing stages:

- RETRO → PRACTICE
- RETRO → FIX

This bypasses learning
and turns retrospectives into reaction rituals.

In this case, the system does not learn.
It reacts.

---

## Final note

RETRO is not where improvement happens.

RETRO is where **reality is recorded**.

Learning begins only after observation is complete.
